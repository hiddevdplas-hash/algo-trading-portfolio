### 2. Multi-pair index cards break the bloc / no-hedge rule over time (serious, tradability)
- **What it is:** a rank card (long the strongest currency, short the weakest) opens new pairs while older positions are still open. The book then holds the same currency both long and short. That is a within-account hedge (FundingPips prohibits it; DESIGN §10 and `CROSS-FIRM-HEDGING.md`) and breaks "one direction per bloc".
- **How often this happens** (extreme-pair rule, h4, 30-bar lookback; positions only, no P&L):
  | hold | bars with some currency both long and short | of which USD | of which JPY |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 bar | 0% | | |
  | 6 bars | 1.2% | | |
  | 30 bars | 55% | 13% | 18% |
- **One-sidedness:** JPY was the weakest currency on 23–30% of h4 bars each year from 2021 to 2024 (12.5% would be uniform). The §6.7 long/short bar is on the pair's quoting side, which means nothing for a cross-sectional card; per currency, the card is a short-JPY trend/carry bet.
- **Smallest fix:**
  - For index cards trading at least 2 pairs, the gatekeeper reports the share of trades opened while the same card holds an opposite position in that currency or bloc. If it is above 1% of trades or above 10% of net R, G1 is recomputed without those entries before the open (the A8.8 guard pattern).
  - Long/short for these cards is also reported per currency exposure (net long/short share per currency, per year).
  - The card states its conflict rule up front.
